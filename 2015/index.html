PV Analyzer I-V Curve Tracer

Renewable energy has grown rapidly in recent years, and photovoltaic solar energy in particular has driven the installation of solar power plants around the world.

That growth created a demand for specific tools and devices that had to evolve at the same pace as the technology.

A key analysis method for a solar power plant is measuring the I-V curve of its modules. Every installation needs at least one device to measure the curve and monitor the cells properly.

The capacitive-load principle is not new, but it had to evolve with current technology to make the system faster, more efficient and easier to use.

As a result, I designed and assembled a new capacitive-load system to meet the market's current needs. It consists of two devices: a PV reference sensor that measures irradiance and temperature, and a PV analyzer/I-V tracer that uses a capacitive load to measure the voltage and current of the selected PV module.

The system meets a real market and industry need in a field that is constantly evolving. It also innovates by using modern technology to create a highly digital, autonomous device that measures, interacts and displays results without needing any other equipment.

The main characteristics to highlight are:

  • Portability of the product, as it can run with the integrated battery without the need of an external power supply, allowing measurements in remote locations.
  • Transmission of irradiance (G) and cell temperature (Tc) by radio, and automatic extrapolation of the measured curve to STC.
  • Accurate measurements, with 12 bits of resolution (4096 values).
  • High autonomy of the batteries, due to the reduced energy consumption.
  • The touchscreen displays the measurements and lets you compare them with the manufacturer's values. Everything can be done in the field and is easy to use.
  • Reduced price compared with similar devices. The assembly cost is around €800. That may seem expensive, but comparable devices are hard to find and those with similar capabilities cost up to $6,000.

Potential users include PV module manufacturers, research institutes and companies involved in the operation and maintenance of solar power plants, regardless of size.
